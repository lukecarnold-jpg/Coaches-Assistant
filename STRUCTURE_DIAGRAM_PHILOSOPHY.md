# Layered Systems

Structure is treated as a field of quiet forces: a small number of precise
nodes, generous space between them, and lines that make relationships visible
before labels explain them. The composition should feel less like a directory
listing and more like an instrument panel for an elegant machine, with the
runtime path acting as its central axis.

Color is used as material, not decoration. Deep navy establishes the ground,
warm ivory carries the structure, and carefully rationed coral, mint, and gold
separate responsibility without fragmenting the whole. Thin rules, measured
corners, and restrained markers create a sense of engineered calm. Every
connection should be crisp enough to feel intentional at a glance.

Scale and rhythm move from the application shell to the supporting systems:
entry point, presentation, domain and persistence, integrations, resources,
tests, and build tooling. Repetition gives the diagram its cadence; small
reference ticks and package labels should reward close inspection without
competing with the main flow. Words remain sparse and functional, integrated
into the geometry rather than presented as prose.

The final artifact must be meticulously crafted, with painstaking attention to
alignment, spacing, line weight, and typographic hierarchy. It should look like
the product of deep expertise and countless hours of refinement: master-level
execution expressed through restraint, clarity, and proportion. Nothing is
ornamental unless it improves orientation; every mark earns its place.
